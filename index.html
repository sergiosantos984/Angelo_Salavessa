The artist

Realism is a discipline of restraint — knowing where not to put ink.

Ângelo Salavessa works in black & grey realism: faces, animals, roses, statues and symbols, rendered in soft gradients that sit naturally on the body.

His home base is Salavessa Tattoo in Alphen aan den Rijn, in the Netherlands. Between sessions there, he takes the machine on the road — Amsterdam, Phuket and Portugal among the stops — and keeps the work going wherever he lands.

Every piece starts with your idea and is composed for the part of you it will live on. Larger work is planned across sessions — to be continued, as he puts it on his feed.

Process

From idea
to skin

No templates, no surprises. Here is how a piece usually comes together — the details are always confirmed with you directly.

  1. 01

    The message

    Send the idea, the placement, a rough size and any references. Short is fine. The form below turns it into one clear WhatsApp message.

    WhatsAppi
  2. 02

    The conversation

    What will actually work on your body: scale, flow, where the light falls on the muscle. Realism needs room — sometimes the honest answer is bigger, or simpler.

    Scale · Flowii
  3. 03

    The design

    The composition is built for you and your placement. Dates, deposits and session length are agreed directly when you book.

    Compositioniii
  4. 04

    The session

    Black & grey is built in layers — from the deepest shadow up to the highlights. Large pieces are split across sessions. To be continued.

    Layersiv
  5. 05

    The healing

    Follow the aftercare you're given. Healed realism softens and settles into the skin — that's the version you live with, and the one that counts.

    Healedv
